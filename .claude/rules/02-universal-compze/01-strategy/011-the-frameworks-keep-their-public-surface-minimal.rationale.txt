# Rationale: Compze's frameworks keep their public surface minimal; its utility libraries may be generous

Added 2026-11-17 on Magnus's word, while the taggregate store spike kept adding public API at JAStudio's request (see the
sidecar of 012, public types are designed for every consumer). The agents had treated the frameworks the way
Compze.Internals.SystemCE is meant to be treated, and the rules said nothing to tell the two apart.

Magnus's words:

- On Compze.Internals.SystemCE: "a swiss army knife of tiny convenience functions with almost universally zero API
  dependencies between each other. The maintenance burden is near zero and surprising the consumer is very unlikely too."
- On the frameworks: "Teventive, Tessaging, TeventStore etc.... Very very complex frameworks that revolutionizes how it
  is possible to build systems. Start treating that like you tread SystemCE and it will become eventually become a
  nightmare to maintain. Imagine if it had thousands or even millions of users when we realized that sorting in these
  methods were a really bad idea.... Not Fun(TM). Same thing for adding a ton of convenient methods that the consumer can
  build themselves. Removing them is No Fun(TM)."
- "So many of our libraries very much go by the principle of less is more. Build the hard stuff and expose it in a way
  that the consumers can easily do the easy stuff without us getting in the way. Keep the interfaces minimal, and keep
  the interfaces C# Interfaces so that we can change anything behind them since the consumer does not even know the
  implementing types...."

The sorting he refers to: the bulk reader's All and an index's Lookup gave their references in id order, about 137 ms and
36 MB a call at a million ids, until both were dropped (see 013, an API spends nothing on what callers can do as well).

Untested beyond the replay `a-consumer-asking-for-an-api-it-could-build-itself` in the catalog's rule-testing, which was
run with 011, 012 and the catalog's 004 changed together.
